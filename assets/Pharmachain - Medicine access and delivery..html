Overview

At Pharmachain, I work as a product designer. I designed the entire website for users to order medicines. We went through rounds of research, wireframing, prototyping, and testing until we arrived at the final product, which is currently in development. I designed and delivered the entire UI and design systems. I worked closely with the founder and engineers, collecting reviews and feedback, guiding the frontend and backend processes, and ensuring that the product met all requirements.

Problem

Pharmachain desired a new website that automates processes from drug search to delivery. The company sought a system that can track and monitor everything, and cater to each user's unique needs. The initial website could not provide these functionalities, so we took the opportunity to redesign all the pages.

The solution

We used the experience gained from the existing website to design a new website that is more visually appealing and focused on the user. We created web applications for various types of users, allowing them to easily create an account, find, and order medicine.
I worked with Pharmachain engineers and PMs to develop the website, which is currently under development. There are many users who have expressed interest in using the product.
Create an account
Users can create an account based on what they do. Pharmachain has four categories of users. It is essential for every user to state which category they belong to in order to receive the best service.
Select medicine
We created a feature where users can select medicine by typing a few keywords. Users are able to add their preferred medicine to a list.
Admin Panel
We also made an admin section for Pharmachain admin to respond to orders, track activities and ensure the functionality of the product.
There are a lot of features included. These are the basic ones.

The process

User Research
Pharmachain’s client/user base changed at a pivotal point in the business history where the focus shifted from individuals (public) to Organizations and professionals (Private). Here are the user we focused the product on:
Hospitals
Health Maintenance Organizations (HMOS)
Healthcare Professionals
Pharmacies
Through various visits and interviews, we were able to establish their needs, frustrations, pain-points and goals.
Existing product Research
Pharmachain began as an idea and grew into a product. Its inception was driven by the CEO's desire to help his sick father find the drugs he needed. Drawing on his background as a pharmacist, he decided to turn this idea into a product that would benefit people around the world.
The existing website was able to collect user requests, but lacked a response and record feature. The homepage was not visually engaging and orders were placed via a Google form that wasn't appealing to users.
The existing website was open to the public, but after months of research, design, and redesign, we transitioned to a business-to-business model. We now directly deal with HMOs, hospitals, and other organizations, allowing us to reach a larger audience.
Certain information from the existing website were brought into the development of this new product. Though, we did a full overhaul.
Sketches and site maps.
In the earlier stages of the design process, we had sketches where we drew samples of what the product will look like, we create site maps, roadmaps etc. Many of these sketches became unusable when we transition into a B2B model. All of the experience was thus incorporated into this new product.
Sitemaps were created on Figjam to help us plan the website in the direction where our solution was focused.
Wireframes and prototypes
I created Wireframes that captured the products needs, they quickly changed into low fidelity designs because we needed to really know what those boxes were and how they link to various parts of the websites. Though, changes were made in the high-fidelity design in light of new Stakeholders requirement and projects constraint.
